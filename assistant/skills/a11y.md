## Rules

- Button labels say where they go and match their action: shop "See our products", visit "Visit us", call "Call
  us", email "Email us", directions "Get directions", telegram "Message us on Telegram"; a button to an `https://`
  address names the place ("Our Facebook page"). Never "Click here", "More", "→" or a label that hides the action.
- Each block's heading says what the block is about in a few plain words, never all in capital letters. The Hero
  holds the page's one main headline (level 1).
- A Heading block's `level` follows the page's outline, not its size: 2 for a heading that starts a part of the
  page, 3 for one under a level-2 heading in the same part (a Section, Columns, Grid or Card), 4 under a 3. Never
  skip a level on the way down (2, then 4). Rich text headings follow the same outline. Change a heading's `size`
  only when the owner asks.
- Picture descriptions (alt text): write only an Image's `alt`, an Image banner's `imageAlt`, the Hero's and Text's
  `imageAlt`, and About's `imageAlt` and `secondImageAlt`. Logos in a Logo strip, Gallery captions and the Hero's
  side-picture labels are the owner's: name them in the reply so the owner fills them in the editor.
- Never guess what a picture shows: you can't see it. Write an alt only from the owner's words about that picture,
  or from text on the page that is clearly about the same picture (its caption, a product name, the block's
  heading). Otherwise leave it as it is and ask in the reply which picture shows what.
- An alt says what the picture shows in a few words (150 characters at most): never "image of", a file name, or a
  list of keywords. Without their alt, the Hero and Text pictures use the heading and About's the caption, so set
  one there only when it says more than that text.
- Whether an Image is decoration only is the owner's choice (its "decorative" switch in the editor). An Image
  banner's alt may stay empty when the words over it say what matters. An Icon whose meaning the words next to it
  don't give gets a short `label`; otherwise its label stays empty.
- Text must be readable against its background (WCAG AA, 4.5:1 for normal text). Fix contrast only through the
  page's colour settings: set a text, background or accent colour override back to null (the palette's own
  colours are readable), or pick another palette. Never invent colours outside the palette.
- With "@" blocks, change only those blocks (and the blocks inside an "@" container); mention a page-wide colour
  problem in the reply instead of fixing it, unless the owner asks.
- Plain words and short sentences help everyone: never leave text that is only symbols or emoji, and spell out an
  abbreviation only when the page or message says what it means (otherwise ask in the reply).
- Never touch props the assistant may not write (customer quotes, Stats numbers, products, the pictures themselves,
  an Image's decorative switch).
